# Podcast Notes — Week 2026-W40

## Risky Business — Risky Bulletin: Authorities dismantle KillSec group (2026-10-02)
- Law enforcement dismantled the KillSec hacking/ransomware group and arrested members across Europe — one of the few recent cases where an active group's operators, not just its infrastructure, were taken into custody, which matters because arrests (versus takedowns alone) tend to have a longer-lasting deterrent effect.
- South African authorities intercepted a ransomware attack aimed at air traffic control systems before encryption occurred, showing that OT/aviation infrastructure is being actively targeted and that early detection in that sector is achievable, not just theoretical.
- A China-linked APT group was observed specifically targeting AI researchers rather than just AI code or model repositories, indicating intellectual-property theft campaigns are pivoting toward the people and research process behind frontier AI, not only its outputs.

[Episode link](https://risky.biz/RBNEWS618/)

## CISO Series / Defense in Depth — Mirror, Mirror on the Wall, Who Has the Best Infrastructure of Them All? (2026-09-29)
- Rivian CISO Mike Johnson argued "determinism doesn't matter, results matter" when defending the use of LLM-based code scanners against the objection that, unlike pattern-matching SAST tools, they don't return identical findings on identical input — a direct challenge to the assumption that non-reproducible AI tool output is unusable as compliance or audit evidence.
- The panel framed every MCP server an organization connects to an AI agent as "an uncontracted vendor," arguing that third-party and vendor-risk processes must extend to these integrations even though they bypass normal procurement and contracting.
- Guest Ryan Lloyd (GuardSquare) raised "architectural vanity" — infrastructure built to match industry best practice or trends rather than actual business need — as an underappreciated driver of unnecessary complexity and cost that audits of infrastructure spend should be probing for.

## Audit, CISO & VAPT angle
The Defense in Depth discussion is the most directly actionable item this week: if LLM-based scanning and MCP-connected AI agents are entering the environment, audit and GRC teams need updated criteria for what counts as acceptable (non-deterministic) tool evidence, and vendor-risk processes need a path to catch MCP/agent integrations that engineering teams adopt outside normal procurement — both are gaps most current third-party-risk programs don't yet cover. The Risky Business items reinforce two VAPT-relevant points worth raising with clients: aviation/OT environments remain live ransomware targets where early-detection controls demonstrably worked, and nation-state actors are now treating AI researchers themselves as a target class, which argues for extending personnel- and research-data-focused threat modeling beyond the usual "protect the model weights" framing.
