# Podcast Notes — Week 2026-W35

## Risky Business — Widespread AI-enabled attacks target Siemens PLCs (2026-08-26)
- Iranian-linked actors hit a UK power generator, and AI-enabled attacks are now hitting Siemens PLCs in US critical infrastructure, prompting joint IC3/CISA alerts — attackers are using AI to speed up ICS-specific exploit development, a niche that used to require specialist offense skills.
- Microsoft patched a "max severity" deserialisation flaw in Entra ID before it was exploited, and a separate Windows IKE Extension RCE was already under active attack — identity infrastructure remains the highest-value, highest-blast-radius target on the network.
- A new phishing kit hijacks passkey enrollment to persist access through password resets, while password-spraying volume against MFA gaps jumped 155x — attackers are adapting quickly to passwordless rollouts rather than being blocked by them.

[Episode link](https://risky.biz/RB850/)

## CISO Series / Defense in Depth — Market Confusion Is Responsible for the Biggest Gaps in Cybersecurity (2026-08-27)
- Panel argued that a crowded, poorly differentiated vendor market is itself a source of defensive gaps: buyers can't cleanly map tools to actual risk, so redundant coverage and blind spots end up coexisting in the same stack.
- The "philosophy problem" segment noted that CISOs and vendors frequently disagree on foundational approach (e.g., prevention-first vs. detection-first) and that the mismatch only surfaces after the contract is signed, wasting a budget cycle.
- Hosts pushed back on the industry habit of "shifting" gap-closing responsibility onto the next layer — tool, team, or leadership — arguing programs need clearer ownership of outcomes rather than diffused accountability.

[Episode link](https://defenseindepth.libsyn.com/market-confusion-is-responsible-for-the-biggest-gaps-in-cybersecurity)

## Cloud Security Podcast — The "Hunt First" AI Security Strategy (2026-08-25)
- Nebulock's founder said 82% of the intrusions his team investigates involve no malware at all, and AI-augmented attacks are up 89% year over year — alert-centric SOCs miss most real intrusions because there's no signature to trigger on.
- AI now lets even single-analyst teams run structured threat hunts over raw, normalized telemetry (endpoint, identity, cloud) instead of just closing alerts; the recommended low-effort starting point is hunting for unapproved/shadow Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers.
- AI agents leave a distinctive behavioral fingerprint — rapid command bursts and breadth-first movement — unlike human attacker pacing, giving defenders a new detection heuristic as agentic attacks and agentic tooling both proliferate.

[Episode link](https://www.cloudsecuritypodcast.tv/videos/the-hunt-first-ai-security-strategy)

## Audit, CISO & VAPT angle
The Siemens PLC targeting and the "Hunt First" MCP-hunting advice point the same direction: this quarter's audit and VAPT scope should explicitly cover AI-agent and MCP inventory alongside OT/ICS assets, since attackers are now automating exploitation of both simultaneously. The passkey-phishing kit that survives password resets is a control worth testing directly in the next VAPT engagement — don't assume a passwordless rollout closes the account-takeover gap. Finally, the Defense in Depth "market confusion" discussion is a governance talking point for leadership: mismatched philosophy between buyer and vendor was named as a root cause of unmonitored control gaps, which is a useful frame when justifying vendor consolidation or new security spend to the board.
