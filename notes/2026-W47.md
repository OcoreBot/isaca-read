# Podcast Notes — Week 2026-W47

## Risky Business — #860: Google: Agentic hacking has "crossed a threshold" (2026-11-18)
- Google's threat intelligence group (GTIG) reports agentic AI threat activity has crossed a significant threshold, including what it calls the first fully automated AI cyberattack by Russian-speaking actors. The FBI issued a flash alert on the ARTEX tool automating attacks at "machine speed", and attackers used AI agents in AWS credential-theft attacks.
- Supply-chain and edge exposure continues: Optimizely confirmed attackers injected malicious code into customer sites, and a PHP2Shell flaw is being chained against unpatched Check Point gateways. A disgruntled researcher dropped a Windows 0-day that gives SYSTEM rights.
- Policy and defence signals: a White House task force argues companies deserve liability relief for AI-fuelled attacks, United plans to block customers' AI agents citing cyber threats, and Anthropic updated its coordinated vulnerability disclosure timelines.

## Cloud Security Podcast — The State of Cloud Security: Why AI Agents Break Deterministic Defense (2026-11-19)
- Palo Alto Networks' 2026 State of Cloud Security report (guest Sarit Tager): about 40% of organisations cannot reconstruct what their AI agents did, and about 73% have given agents write access to pipelines or configurations.
- Agents lack a single identity (service accounts, borrowed user tokens, SaaS credentials), and shadow AI (skills, MCP servers, agents) often goes undetected. Pattern-based hunting and attack-path analysis may miss non-deterministic agent behaviour.
- Patching cannot keep pace with frontier-model vulnerability discovery, so teams are moving to runtime sensors, virtual patching and compensating controls. Customers are more comfortable with auto-remediation in code than at runtime, and CloudSec, AppSec and SOC boundaries are blurring.

[Episode link](https://www.cloudsecuritypodcast.tv/videos/the-state-of-cloud-security-why-ai-agents-break-deterministic-defense-palo-alto-networks)

## Audit, CISO & VAPT angle
AI agent governance is the common thread: auditors should ask clients for an inventory of agents, their identities and permissions, and whether agent actions are logged well enough to reconstruct. Leadership and board discussions should cover machine-speed attacks, the compressed patch window, and the emerging liability debate. For VAPT, add agent-driven attack simulation, chained edge-device exploits (such as Check Point gateways) and credential theft in cloud environments to scope, and test whether detection works at machine speed.
