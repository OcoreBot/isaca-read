## CISO Series / Defense in Depth — Can SMBs Get the Same Security as Everyone Else? (October 8, 2026)
- David Spark and Steve Zalewski, with ThreatDown's Mark Stockley, argue weak security at small and mid-sized businesses isn't just an SMB problem — it ripples through the supply chains and ecosystems larger enterprises depend on.
- They frame vendor neglect of SMBs as a chicken-and-egg economics problem: security vendors chase enterprise deals because that's where the margin is, leaving SMBs under-served by tooling built for their scale and budget.
- The panel pushes secure-by-default configuration as the practical fix, since SMBs can't sustain constant manual security upkeep — and they're clear that outsourcing to an MSP or vendor doesn't transfer accountability for the outcome.

## Audit, CISO & VAPT angle
This week's one fresh episode — Defense in Depth's SMB security discussion — is directly useful for third-party and supply-chain risk work: if a client's vendor or acquisition pipeline includes SMBs, their under-resourced security posture is a ripple-effect risk worth scoping explicitly rather than assuming "small vendor, small risk." The secure-by-default argument also translates into an audit talking point: when reviewing control design for smaller business units or subsidiaries, push for defaults that don't depend on sustained manual diligence, and don't let "we use a managed provider" close out an accountability finding — ownership of the risk stays with the business regardless of who operates the tooling.
