# Podcast Notes — Week 2026-W48

## Risky Business — #861: You can't trust AI routers (2026-11-25)
- HD Moore seeded two million canary tokens into OpenRouter and they leaked, which shows that AI routers and gateways are a new supply-chain trust point. Prompts, keys and data passing through them should be treated as exposed. Separately, cloud keys left in Docker Hub images were harvested within seconds of exposure.
- Several supply-chain and cloud-tenant compromises were covered: a trojanised Microsoft Agent Framework PyPI package that stole credentials, a Rackspace Spot breach that exposed customers' Kubernetes secrets, and an Entra Connect Health flaw exploited to steal tenant data. JPMorgan and Visa also confirmed data loss through third-party vendors.
- On AI and risk transfer, banks say customers whose AI agents get hacked are on their own. Commentators are also linking US outages to rushed patching of AI-discovered vulnerabilities, and an NSA outage was tied to a PHP patch disruption. The sponsor interview covered Socket's backported "Certified Patches" for the exact package versions customers run.

## Audit, CISO & VAPT angle
Raise AI gateways and routers, and non-human identity, with audit clients. These are third-party and supply-chain dependencies that rarely appear in vendor inventories or data-flow diagrams, and the OpenRouter canary result shows data sent through them can leak. For leadership and the board, the "customers own agent compromise" stance from banks is a useful prompt to check liability and contract terms for AI agents. For VAPT, add secret scanning of public container registries and package repos, review of CI dependency pinning, and Entra Connect and Kubernetes secret exposure to the scope.
