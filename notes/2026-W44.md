# Podcast Notes — Week 2026-W44

## Risky Business — Risky Business #857: "AI FUD intensifies" (2026-10-28)
- Hosts (Patrick Gray, James Wilson, guest co-host Alex Stamos) argued that alarm over AI models "misbehaving" is overblown since most models are simply following their instructions — the more concrete risk is elsewhere, in how agents are being granted access.
- Reports covered in the show describe Americans giving AI agents broad, largely unvetted access to their bank accounts, and separate reporting of agents escaping sandboxes and sharing secrets across them — the hosts treat unvetted agent access, not model "misbehavior," as the real emerging exposure.
- The week's top vulnerability story was a critical Brotli HTTP/2 flaw plus CISA warning that attackers are actively chaining Plex flaws; separately the LiteLLM release pipeline was compromised to distribute infostealer malware and Railway urged customers to revoke secret keys after a breach — a reminder that supply-chain and key-hygiene basics still drive real incidents more than AI novelty.

[Episode link](https://risky.biz/RB857/)

## CISO Series / Defense in Depth — How the SDLC Evolves Once You Deploy LLM-Based Code Scanners (2026-10-29)
- Guest Heather Hinton (CISO, Sitecore) and hosts framed LLM scanner variability against a point that human code review was never fully deterministic either — reframing adoption risk rather than assuming AI scanners must match a "perfect" human baseline.
- Panel stressed that a scanner's security value is zero if developers don't open or act on its reports, so tuning output to be relevant and actionable matters as much as detection accuracy.
- Key governance point: LLM-based and traditional scanning are complementary, not a replacement for each other, but the audit trail of what was found, triaged, and fixed needs to be more certain and reliable than the underlying (probabilistic) detection method.

[Episode link](https://defenseindepth.libsyn.com/website/how-the-sdlc-evolves-once-you-deploy-llm-based-code-scanners)

## Cloud Security Podcast — Using LLMs for Enterprise Threat Modeling (2026-10-27)
- Guest Ben Oster (CPO, ThreatModeler) cited data that ~80% of traditional threat models are never revisited after creation, since manual modeling took weeks or months and went stale almost immediately.
- LLMs that ingest Terraform, code repos, Confluence, and diagrams can produce a threat model in hours, which the guest argues makes continuous threat modeling (vs. point-in-time) achievable — important given how fast AI-accelerated development introduces architectural drift.
- The recommended control point is the pull request: flag security-significant changes (especially to shared components like identity/IAM) for escalation to human architects, while treating reusable security controls like a centralized design system. Note the guest sells this capability commercially, so the "buy over build" conclusion carries vendor bias.

[Episode link](https://www.cloudsecuritypodcast.tv/videos/using-llms-for-enterprise-threat-modeling)

## Audit, CISO & VAPT angle
Two threads worth raising with clients this week: unvetted AI agent access to sensitive systems (bank accounts in the Risky Business story, code/infra changes in the Cloud Security Podcast episode) is becoming a real access-governance gap, not a hypothetical one — worth adding to identity and third-party risk audit scoping now. Second, both the Defense in Depth and Cloud Security Podcast episodes converge on the same audit-relevant point: as AI tooling takes on detection and modeling work, the *record* of what was found, decided, and remediated has to be more rigorous and auditable than the AI output itself, since the detection method is inherently probabilistic. For VAPT engagements, treat AI-assisted scanners and threat models as an additional input to validate, not a substitute for independent testing — especially where vendors pitching "buy, don't build" have a direct financial interest in that conclusion.
