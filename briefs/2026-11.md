# Journal & Research Brief — 2026-11

## ISACA Journal
Current issue is **Volume 5, 2026: "The Fundamentals of Trust."** Most relevant picks:
- [Moving From Heat Maps to Dollars: A Guide to Financial Cyberrisk Quantification](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/issues/2026/volume-5/moving-from-heat-maps-to-dollars-a-guide-to-financial-cyberrisk-quantification) — makes the case for quantifying cyberrisk in dollar terms instead of red/amber/green heat maps, useful ammunition for board reporting.
- [Beyond Cryptography: The Strategic Dilemma of Post-Quantum Readiness](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/issues/2026/volume-5/beyond-cryptography-the-strategic-dilemma-of-post-quantum-readiness) — frames PQC migration as a strategic/governance decision, not just a crypto-library swap.
- [On Auditing MCP Deployments](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/issues/2026/volume-5/on-auditing-mcp-deployments) — practical angle on auditing Model Context Protocol integrations as agentic AI tooling spreads.
- [AI Augmentation and Audit's Transparency Gap](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/issues/2026/volume-5/ai-augmentation-and-audits-transparency-gap) — on documenting and disclosing AI assistance used during audit work itself.
- [The Role of Foundational Trust in Evolving Access Control](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/issues/2026/volume-5/the-role-of-foundational-trust-in-evolving-access-control) — rethinking access control models as identity becomes the primary trust anchor.

Full text is member-only; abstracts and the article pages above are public.

## Standards & frameworks
- **COBIT 7** — ISACA's campaign page ([isaca.org/campaigns/cobit-7-teaser](https://www.isaca.org/campaigns/cobit-7-teaser)) now says the framework is "Coming November 2026," pushing past the October 27 date widely reported by training vendors. It marks COBIT's 30th anniversary, adds a digital platform, three-tiered assessments, and an updated Design Guide, and touches on "emerging risks, including AI." No official word yet on a COBIT 2019 retirement timeline — confirm before advising clients ([isaca.org](https://www.isaca.org/resources/news-and-trends/newsletters/atisaca/2026/volume-8/celebrating-three-decades-of-cobit)).
- **SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria** — AICPA's SOC 2 Working Group proposed the first major restructuring in years, adding points of focus for AI governance, model monitoring, prompt manipulation, data poisoning, and automated-decision risk. Comment period runs through January 15, 2027; if adopted, applies to engagements beginning on/after December 31, 2027 ([Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2026/oct/aicpa-proposes-improvements-to-soc-2-trust-services-criteria/)).
- **ISO/IEC 27000:2026** — sixth edition of the foundational vocabulary/overview standard released July 2026; not certifiable itself, but 27001/27002 are expected to progressively realign with it over time ([DQS Global](https://www.dqsglobal.com/en/explore/blog/iso-iec-27000-2026-released)). No confirmed ISO/IEC 27001:2026 edition — treat claims of one as unverified.
- **CIS Controls / PCI DSS** — nothing new this month. CIS Controls remains at v8.1; PCI DSS remains at v4.0.1 with no v5.0 announcement found.

## NIST watch
- [SP 800-100 Rev. 1 (initial public draft)](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/100/r1/ipd) — rewritten "Information Security Handbook" for executive leadership, realigned to CSF 2.0. Comments due **December 4, 2026**.
- [SP 800-221 Rev. 1 (initial public draft)](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/221/r1/ipd) — guidance on feeding ICT risk registers into enterprise risk management under CSF 2.0. Comments due **December 7, 2026**.
- [SP 800-82 Rev. 4 (initial public draft)](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r4/ipd) — major OT/ICS security guide update, now built around CSF 2.0 and zero-trust architecture. Comments due **November 30, 2026** — relevant for anyone with ICS/OT scope in upcoming fieldwork.
- [SP 800-185 Rev. 1 (initial public draft)](https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-185/rev-1/draft) — update to SHA-3 derived functions (cSHAKE, KMAC, TupleHash, ParallelHash).
- [CSWP 36G (initial public draft)](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/cswp/36/g/ipd) and [IR 8623 (initial public draft)](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8623/ipd) — 5G false base station and O-RAN CSF 2.0 community profile drafts, both open for comment.

## Reports worth reading
- **CSA, The VulnOps Operating Model** (Oct 27, 2026) — argues batch-style vulnerability management can't keep pace with AI-accelerated vulnerability discovery and patch generation; pushes toward continuous, workflow-driven vuln ops ([cloudsecurityalliance.org](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/the-vulnops-operating-model)).
- **CSA, The State of Data Privacy Engineering** (Oct 21, 2026) — maps where privacy engineering practice is struggling against compliance and emerging-tech pressure ([cloudsecurityalliance.org](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/the-state-of-data-privacy-engineering)).
- **CSA, DLP in the AI Era** survey report (Oct 9, 2026) — finds traditional DLP programs are being bypassed via generative AI tools, cloud storage, and unmanaged devices ([cloudsecurityalliance.org](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/dlp-in-the-ai-era-survey-report)).
- ENISA's annual Threat Landscape 2026 report landed just before this window (September 2026) and is still worth a read if you haven't — no October follow-up update found ([enisa.europa.eu](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/ENISA%20Threat%20Landscape%202026_Final.pdf)).

## What to act on
- Flag the SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria exposure draft to clients heading into 2027 SOC 2 renewals — the AI-related points of focus will likely shape what auditors ask for; comment window closes January 15, 2027.
- If OT/ICS is in scope for upcoming fieldwork, read the SP 800-82 Rev. 4 draft before finalizing the control checklist — comments close November 30, 2026.
- Hold off on retiring COBIT 2019 materials or committing to a COBIT 7 transition date until ISACA confirms the actual launch date and retirement timeline (campaign page still says "Coming November 2026").
