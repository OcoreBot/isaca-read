# Security & Audit Digest — 2026-08-11

## Top items

### 1. SonicWall SMA1000 Flaws Now Exploited by Ransomware Gangs
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** CISA-confirmed active exploitation of a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) SSRF flaw in a VPN gateway appliance is an urgent remote-access patch/verify checkpoint.
Ransomware affiliates are exploiting two recently patched SonicWall SMA1000 vulnerabilities, including the SSRF bug, to compromise VPN appliances and gain initial network access. ([source](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cisa-sonicwall-sma1000-flaws-now-exploited-by-ransomware-gangs/))

### 2. CISA #StopRansomware Advisory: Gunra Ransomware-as-a-Service
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** A formal CISA advisory documenting affiliate TTPs against government and critical-infrastructure targets — useful for incident-response playbooks and audit control mapping.
CISA published joint advisory AA26-222A detailing Gunra RaaS operations, which affiliates use to target government, critical infrastructure, and other organizations. ([source](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-222a))

### 3. StormEncryptor Ransomware — New Strain from China-Linked Storm-1175
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** Microsoft says the former Medusa affiliate likely used the N-Central RMM flaw (covered previously) as an entry point, reinforcing MSP supply-chain risk as an active ransomware vector.
Storm-1175, a financially motivated China-linked actor, deployed a previously undocumented ransomware strain, StormEncryptor, marking a tooling shift from its earlier Medusa usage. ([source](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/china-linked-hackers-deploy-new.html))

### 4. New Passkey Attacks Recover Synced Keys, Bypass Phishing-Resistant MFA
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** Three independent research efforts show signed authentication material can be reused to defeat passkeys without breaking the underlying crypto — a direct challenge to assumptions behind passkey rollouts.
The attacks target synced private-key material and MFA workflows rather than cryptographic weaknesses, meaning existing passkey deployments may need compensating controls. ([source](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/new-passkey-attacks-can-recover-synced.html))

### 5. LexisNexis Shuts Down Services After Suspicious Server Activity
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** A data broker holding sensitive due-diligence and background-check data going offline over suspected compromise is a third-party-risk red flag for any client relying on its feeds.
LexisNexis took its Diligence, Metabase API, and Newsdesk services offline after detecting unusual activity on servers managed by an unnamed third-party vendor. ([source](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/lexisnexis-shuts-down-services-after-suspicious-activity-on-servers/))

### 6. Valve Notifies Steam Hardware Customers of Data Breach via Logistics Partner
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** A textbook vendor-risk lesson — the breach originated at shipping partner CEVA Logistics, not Valve itself, underscoring the need for third-party-risk assessments to cover fulfillment vendors.
Valve is notifying European Steam hardware customers that attackers stole their data after compromising CEVA Logistics, Valve's shipping partner. ([source](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/valve-notifies-steam-hardware-customers-of-a-data-breach/))

### 7. Kimsuky Builds Offline AI Stack to Automate Phishing and Malware Development
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** State-sponsored actors moving from public chatbot prompting to self-hosted LLMs signals AI-assisted threat tooling is maturing — relevant to detection engineering and AI-governance risk assessments.
North Korea's Kimsuky group is now running AI models offline on its own infrastructure, linking document-search tools to stolen files to speed phishing content and malware development. ([source](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/kimsuky-builds-offline-ai-stack-that.html))
