# Security & Audit Digest — 2026-08-20

## Top items

### 1. CISA adds MLflow SSRF flaw to KEV catalog, federal deadline September 2
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** A formal KEV addition with a hard remediation date gives audit teams a concrete control-test deadline, and confirms MLflow SSRF exploitation (already flagged by researchers) is now officially active in the wild.
CISA added CVE-2026-64849, an MLflow Server-Side Request Forgery vulnerability, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog based on evidence of active exploitation, with federal agencies required to remediate by 2026-09-02. ([source](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/19/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog))

### 2. CISA warns of active, AI-generated attacks on Siemens S7 PLCs
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** Attackers using AI-generated scripts against OT/PLC targets raises the bar for ICS segmentation audits and signals a broader trend beyond Siemens — VAPT scoping for critical-infrastructure clients should add PLC-specific test cases now.
CISA and partner agencies issued an advisory on an active threat to Siemens S7 Series PLCs, warning that threat actors are using AI-generated scripts to exploit them in U.S. critical infrastructure, and noting the targeting activity extends beyond Siemens devices. ([source](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-231a))

### 3. Password spraying attacks surge 155x as attackers exploit MFA gaps
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** A direct control-failure lesson — legacy authentication protocols and inconsistent MFA enforcement are the exploited gap, making this a priority finding for identity/access audits and a standard VAPT test case.
Huntress reported a 155x increase in password spraying in H1 2026, including one campaign generating more than 81 million login attempts in two weeks by exploiting legacy authentication and unprotected MFA policy gaps. ([source](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/password-spraying-attacks-surge-155x-as-hackers-exploit-mfa-gaps/))

### 4. Medusa ransomware has breached 500+ US critical infrastructure orgs since 2021
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** Confirms a long-running, high-volume threat to critical-infrastructure sectors — useful benchmark data for board-level risk reporting and for prioritizing ransomware-readiness audits in those sectors.
The FBI and CISA said the Medusa ransomware gang has breached more than 500 US critical infrastructure organizations since June 2021, underscoring sustained targeting of essential services. ([source](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cisa-medusa-ransomware-hit-over-500-critical-infrastructure-orgs/))

### 5. Cloudflare Workers Spectre attack leaks JWTs from co-located workers
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** A concrete side-channel proof-of-concept against serverless multi-tenancy — VAPT teams testing shared-infrastructure workloads should account for this, and CISOs relying on Cloudflare Workers should ask about isolation guarantees.
Researchers disclosed a remote Spectre attack against Cloudflare Workers that leaked a JWT from a co-located Worker in production at up to 12 bits/second — 360x faster than a prior demonstration. ([source](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/cloudflare-workers-spectre-attack-leaks.html))

### 6. 14,500+ Dahua devices compromised via credential attacks and auth bypass
**Why it matters for audit/CISO/VAPT:** Demonstrates how credential reuse plus unpatched auth-bypass CVEs plus P2P relay features combine into a scalable device-compromise chain — a good template for VAPT test plans on IoT/camera fleets and for audit patch-verification checks.
Hunt.io disclosed a campaign that compromised over 14,530 Dahua devices between June 17 and July 22, 2026, using credential attacks, two authentication-bypass flaws, and a peer-to-peer relay technique, concentrated in Ukraine and Russia. ([source](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/hackers-compromised-14500-dahua-devices.html))
